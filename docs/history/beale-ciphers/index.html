What Are the Beale Ciphers?

The Beale ciphers are three strings of numbers published with a buried treasure story in 1885. One has a claimed solution. The other two remain unsolved, and the story has never been proved true.

Updated 2 September 2026

Short answer: The Beale ciphers are not verified directions to a real treasure. They are numerical cryptograms printed in an 1885 pamphlet called The Beale Papers. The pamphlet claims that Thomas J. Beale buried a fortune in Bedford County, Virginia. No treasure has been authenticated. Researchers still dispute whether Beale and his story were real.

Where did the story come from?

The story begins with the pamphlet. There is no supporting collection of early nineteenth-century records. Its narrator says that Beale left a locked box with Robert Morriss, an innkeeper in Lynchburg. The box supposedly held letters and three coded texts. One gave the location of the vault. Another described the treasure. The third named its owners.

Which cipher was decoded?

Cipher 2 has a published solution based on the United States Declaration of Independence. The decoded text describes deposits of precious metal and jewels. It says cipher 1 gives the exact location. No generally accepted solution exists for ciphers 1 or 3.

Important distinction: a decoded message does not prove that Beale or his treasure existed.

Why do people suspect a hoax?

Researchers have found problems with the dates and language in the pamphlet. They have also questioned the lack of supporting records. Cryptographers disagree about whether the remaining number strings contain meaningful text. The evidence has never established the pamphlet’s central claim.

How the mystery enters the novel

Tianna and the Mystery of the Beale Treasure is historical fiction inspired by the pamphlet and its unsolved codes. Tianna and her family are fictional. So are the journal, the Celestial Mirror and the investigation. The book does not claim to reveal a historical solution.
